# Deck for Gmail: a chief of staff for your inbox

Deck reads every email, tells you what matters, drafts your replies in your voice, follows up for you and keeps your inbox clean. It works all day, even when you are not looking. Nothing goes out without your rules.

This is the target product post for the Gmail beta. The owner approved it on 2026-11-21. It describes the end goal, not the current state. See [gmail-product-plan.md](gmail-product-plan.md) for the plan.

Email is still where work, money, travel and family plans arrive. It is also where they get lost. Most of us spend the first hour of the day sorting, not deciding. Filters break, newsletters pile up, and the one email that mattered is on page three.

Deck gives you an AI assistant with one job: to run your Gmail the way a great chief of staff would. It knows who matters to you. It knows what each email asks of you. It answers the easy things, prepares the hard things, and asks you only when your judgement is really needed.

**The promise:** five minutes each morning to be on top of every inbox you own. Zero emails forgotten. Nothing sent, deleted or paid without the permission you chose.

## Everything Deck does

### 1. Know what matters

- **Smart triage.** Every email is sorted as it arrives: *Needs reply*, *To do*, *Waiting on others*, *FYI* or *Noise*.
- **Priority people.** Deck learns who matters from how you act: who you reply to fast, who you star, who you ignore. You can also pin your VIPs.
- **Urgent alerts.** A security alert, a failed payment, a flight change or a message from a VIP reaches your phone at once. Everything else waits for your brief.
- **Labels that make sense.** Deck labels mail in Gmail by group and by topic, such as Receipts, Travel or Clients. Your own labels and filters stay as they are.

### 2. Your daily brief

- One brief for all your accounts, at the times you choose: morning, evening or both.
- Delivered where you are: the Deck app, push notification, email, Slack, WhatsApp or Telegram.
- Each item has a one-line summary and one-tap actions: *Approve draft*, *Reply*, *Snooze*, *Archive*, *Open in Gmail*.
- A weekly review: what you handled, what is still open, how much noise Deck removed and how much time you saved.

### 3. Replies in your voice

- Deck drafts a reply for every email that needs one. The draft waits in Gmail and in Deck for you to approve.
- It learns your tone from your sent mail: short with colleagues, warm with family, formal with clients.
- It uses context: earlier threads, attachments and facts you taught it, such as your rates, your address or your availability.
- For routine mail that you allow, such as “thanks, received” or a meeting confirmation, Deck can send on its own and tell you after.

### 4. Never drop a thread

- **Follow-ups.** When you send an email that needs an answer, Deck watches it. No answer by the date you set? Deck reminds you, or sends a polite nudge for you.
- **Snooze.** “Bring this back on Monday” or “when the payment arrives”.
- **Promises you made.** If you write “I'll send it by Friday”, Deck adds it to your to-do list and reminds you on Thursday.

### 5. A clean inbox, kept clean

- **Inbox cleanup.** Deck works through years of old mail and sorts, archives or trashes it by your rules. It shows you a plan before the first big cleanup.
- **Subscription manager.** See every newsletter and mailing list, with how often it arrives and whether you ever open it. Unsubscribe, keep, or send to a weekly digest with one tap.
- **Newsletter digest.** The newsletters you keep become one short read, with the key points and links.
- **Noise removal.** Promotions and cold sales emails go to Trash or a quiet label, by your choice.

### 6. Just ask your inbox

You: What did the accountant ask me for last month?

Deck: Three things: your Form 16, the rent receipts for April to June, and your bank statement for June. You sent the Form 16 on 12 Oct. The other two are still open. Want me to draft a reply with the rent receipts attached?

You: Yes. And from now on, label anything from her as Taxes.

Deck: Done. Draft is ready for review. New rule saved: mail from your accountant → `Taxes`.

- Search your whole mail history in plain words, across all accounts.
- Give orders in plain words: reply, forward, archive, find, sum up a long thread.
- Make rules in plain words. They replace fragile Gmail filters.

### 7. Your money, trips and deliveries, in one place

- **Bills and payments.** Deck finds bills, due dates and amounts, and reminds you before they are due. A payment that already went through is filed, not flagged.
- **Receipts and invoices.** Saved to a folder you choose, ready for taxes or expense claims, with a monthly export.
- **Travel.** Flights, hotels and bookings become one trip summary. Changes reach you at once.
- **Deliveries.** Every order and its status, without opening ten shipping emails.
- **Meetings.** Invites are noticed, clashes are flagged, and you accept or decline from your brief.

### 8. Attachments handled

- Deck reads attachments: PDFs, invoices, contracts and statements. It uses what they say in summaries and replies.
- It saves the files you care about to the folder or drive you choose, with clear names.
- It attaches the right file when it drafts a reply.

## You decide how much Deck does

Every action has a level of trust that you set. You can change it at any time, per action and per account.

| Level | What Deck does | Good for |
|----|----|----|
| Suggest | Prepares the action and waits for you. | Replies to clients, anything with money |
| Ask once | Asks the first few times, then offers to do it alone. | New rules, unsubscribes |
| Autopilot | Acts and tells you in the brief. | Labels, receipts, noise, routine confirmations |

Deck starts on *Suggest* for everything. It earns more trust as you approve its work. When you correct it (“this is not spam”, “this person is important”), it learns and does better next time.

### Always reversible

- **Activity log.** Every action Deck took, why it took it, and the email it was about.
- **Undo.** Any label, archive, move or cleanup can be undone from the log.
- **Pause.** One switch stops all actions. Deck keeps reading so that nothing is missed.
- **Watch it work.** Open Deck and see live what your assistant is doing.

## Safe by design

- **Emails cannot give orders.** Deck treats every email as information, never as an instruction. An email that says “send me your password” or “delete all mail” changes nothing.
- **Guard rails that never move.** Deck never deletes mail for good, never empties Trash and never pays or buys anything. It never sends to a new contact, never shares a password or code, and never forwards a security email unless you approve it each time.
- **Your sign-in stays yours.** Your password goes straight to your assistant's private, encrypted computer. The AI never sees it. Deck keeps you signed in, and it tells you if Google asks you to sign in again.
- **Your data, your choice.** Use Deck in the cloud, or host it on your own machine. Your mail is not used to train AI models. Export or delete everything with one click.
- **Clear spending.** You set a monthly limit. Deck shows its cost per day and never goes past your limit.

**Every Gmail account**Personal Gmail and Google Workspace. Connect as many as you need. One brief covers all of them.

**Fast**New mail is sorted within seconds. Urgent mail reaches you at once.

**Your whole history**Inbox, archive and every label. Not just the last few days.

**Any language**Deck reads, sums up and replies in the language of each email.

**Your time zone**Briefs and reminders follow your local time, including daylight saving and travel.

**Desktop and phone**A full desktop app and a phone app with notifications and one-tap approvals.

## Up and running in five minutes

1.  Tell Deck what you want, in a sentence: “Keep my inboxes clean and brief me at 8.”
2.  Connect your Gmail accounts. Sign in once, safely.
3.  Deck reads your recent mail and shows you what it found: your VIPs, your subscriptions, your open threads and a cleanup plan.
4.  Choose your trust levels, your brief times and your monthly limit.
5.  Get your first brief within the hour.
